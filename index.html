Why Banking

When I was 13, I sold cookies in my village to save enough for a bicycle. I did not understand margins, customer behavior, or strategy. I only learned that selling something does not automatically make a business work, and I have wanted to understand why some businesses work and others do not ever since.

I started college interested in entrepreneurship and seriously considered consulting, because I liked working through business problems. Citi's Freshman Discovery Program then introduced me to investment banking, capital markets, and the role financial institutions play in major corporate decisions.

At KSL Capital Partners, I completed a guided three-statement modeling workshop and studied portfolio-company businesses and investment processes. That was a learning exercise, not deal work, but it showed me how much of a company's story shows up in its financial statements. I have kept building on it through economics coursework, the Lewis & Clark Investment Club, and a mentorship with a JPMorgan professional through Out for Undergrad.

What draws me to investment banking is where several things overlap: understanding a business, analyzing its financial statements, thinking about valuation, and advising on decisions like raising capital or pursuing an acquisition. M&A in particular puts a company at a moment of change, and the team has to understand it quickly and well.

I know I am early in this process. Investment banking appeals to me because it offers rigorous training at the start of a career and a close look at how companies make consequential decisions. I would rather learn that by doing the work than by guessing from the outside.
